How the rating works

Solunar periods. John Alden Knight's solunar theory says fish and game feed hardest when the moon is directly overhead or directly underfoot (major periods, about two hours) and when it rises or sets (minor periods, about an hour). The page computes moon and sun positions for your coordinates and finds those moments.

Moon phase. Periods count for more within a few days of a new or full moon, when the sun and moon pull together and tides run strongest. The day rating dots go up when that happens and when a period lands on sunrise or sunset.

Species habits. Every species has its own activity curve for dawn, daytime, dusk and night, a weight for how much it responds to solunar periods, a response to moonlight at night, a seasonal curve (flipped automatically for the southern hemisphere), and a preferred water temperature range. A walleye's best hour and a pike's best hour on the same day can be far apart.

Geography. Each species has a fit score for each kind of spot. Mulloway live on deep drops, river mouths and rock walls; bream on oyster racks and walls; tailor in the surf and off headlands; cod in deep snags. Fishing the right structure keeps the full rating, average structure trims it, and the wrong water (a cod in the surf) drops it near zero. Pick "Not sure" to rate on timing alone and see where each fish likes to be.

Where they live. Each species carries its known range, native and introduced, including small or rare populations. Only species found at your coordinates are listed.

Live conditions. Water temperature, air (barometric) pressure, its trend and the swell all come from the Open-Meteo forecast for the spot, hour by hour, up to about two weeks ahead. Sea temperature is the forecast surface temperature; lakes and rivers use the last 30 days of air temperature, which water follows closely. When live data isn't available (too far ahead, or the preview page), the rating falls back to a seasonal estimate and says so.

Swell. Each saltwater species has a swell range it likes: tailor and mulloway want some whitewater, snapper a moderate sea, flathead and bream calm water. How much swell matters depends on the spot: a lot on a surf beach or rock wall, little up an estuary, not at all in fresh water. Big swell on exposed rocks is flagged as dangerous.

Air pressure. Fish feel pressure changes through their swim bladder. A falling barometer before a front often triggers a feeding spell, steady pressure is normal, and the clear, rising high after a front usually slows the bite. Species that live shallow in fresh water (bass, pike, walleye) react most; saltwater fish follow the tide more than the barometer. The page reads the trend from the change over the previous three hours.

The number. The rating blends the best two-hour stretch of the day with the overall quality of the top six hours, then scales by season, spot type and water temperature. Pressure and swell are applied hour by hour, so they move the best windows as well as the score. 75 and up is excellent, 55 good, 35 fair. It is a planning guide, not a promise; weather fronts, pressure, wind and tides (for salt water) also matter and aren't included yet.
